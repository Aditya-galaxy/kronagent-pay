$1,500 PAID FOR 845,000 VIEWS THAT WERE 99.999% BOTS

That is one brand, on one campaign, on an existing creator payout platform. It is not an outlier — it is what happens when a platform pays on a number the person being paid can buy for less than the payout.

The incumbents take a percentage of what brands spend. Read that again: when a brand pays for bots, the platform earns more. Nobody in that arrangement is paid to care.

So we changed what's being bought. Not the detection — the product.

The dwell window

Platforms remove inauthentic views, usually within a day. So we don't pay on views. We pay on views that survived — and we wait to find out which.

1

Post the clip

A creator submits a clip against an open campaign. The rate, dwell window and cap are frozen right then, and the brand can't move them afterwards.

2

Wait 24 hours

We read the view count from the platform, never from the creator. Then we read it again a day later.

3

Pay what's left

You're paid on the lower of the two counts, in USDC, straight to your wallet. Bought views are gone by then. Yours aren't.

campaign agent — hourly pass PAID clip 4f2a 62,400 views held for 24h → 62.40 USDC PAID clip 91be 18,900 views held for 24h → 18.90 USDC HELD clip c07d dwell window not elapsed — 9h to go HELD clip 3ea1 count fell 812,447 → 53 overnight BLOCKED clip 55cf campaign pool exhausted the creator who actually got watched is paid first, not loudest.

A falling count isn't punished — it just isn't paid. Views that never survived were never earned.

One mechanic, two problems

The same rule protects both sides of the deal, which is why neither side has to trust the other.

For creators

Get paid, without asking twice

The complaint isn't rates. It's chasing an invoice for six weeks and being told the terms changed.

  • Terms freeze when your clip is accepted — the brand cannot lower your rate afterwards
  • Paid in USDC to your own wallet, usually minutes after the window closes
  • No contest, no placing — everyone who delivers surviving views gets paid
  • The remaining pool is public before you start, so you know if it's worth the edit
For brands

Stop buying deleted views

You already can't tell which half of the campaign was real. This makes that question irrelevant.

  • You are only ever charged for views that outlived the dwell window
  • Hard pool ceiling and per-creator caps — the agent cannot exceed either
  • Every decision is written to an append-only log you can verify yourself
  • We charge per campaign and per settled payout — never a cut of your spend

We deliberately don't take a percentage of spend. A percentage means our revenue goes up when you pay for bots — the exact conflict that produced the $1,500 above.

What holds it together

No clawback

Settled money stays settled

USDC can't be recalled, so we don't pretend otherwise. If a count falls after we've paid, the next payout goes to zero instead — the creator is never chased for money already in their wallet.

Frozen terms

The brand can't move the goalposts

Rate, dwell and cap are captured at acceptance and stored with the submission. Changing the campaign later changes nothing about work already accepted.

Agent leash

The AI proposes, the engine disposes

An agent sets rates and investigates suspicious growth — but a rate outside the operator's band is refused, not clamped, and the investigator's strongest verdict is "wait". Nothing the model can return releases money.

Evidence

An append-only record

Every snapshot, decision and payout is one immutable event. The hash chain is derived on read, so altering any past entry breaks verification of every entry after it.

What this is not

  • Not a bot detector. We don't claim to out-detect fraud farms. We changed what gets bought so that outlasting them isn't necessary.
  • Not a marketplace. We don't match brands to creators or take a finder's fee. We're the payout engine a campaign runs on.
  • Not instant. The dwell window is the product. If you want to be paid the second you post, this is the wrong platform — and that's deliberate.
  • Early. Settlement is proven on testnet, not at scale. You would be among the first campaigns.
